Use evidence—not instinct alone

Territory strategy is resource allocation. Your goal is not to label customers as “good” or “bad,” but to decide where limited selling effort is most likely to create meaningful mutual value. Scores are a starting point; your written justification matters most.

Segment Your Prospects

Define a useful group, then see which of your prospects actually fit it. You can identify more than one prospect.

Quick reminder: What makes a useful segment?

A segment groups customers who share a meaningful characteristic that changes how you serve, approach, or prioritize them. It should be identifiable, reachable, meaningfully different, and actionable—not simply a list of your favorite accounts.

Pick an approach to see what evidence to look for.

Consider your ability to reach and serve the group, your capabilities, and the customer outcome you can support.

Now apply the segment to your prospects

In the next section, use each prospect card's Segment fit dropdown. Choose Fits, Possibly, Does not fit, or Not enough evidence. More than one prospect can fit the same segment, and you should only claim a match when you have a reason.

Compare Five Customer Opportunities

Use the same criteria for every customer. Score each from 1 (low) to 5 (high).

Scoring guide: Strategic fit = alignment with company priorities; growth = future potential; relationship value = retention, referrals, credibility, insight, and access; probability = realistic likelihood of progress; resource efficiency = value relative to time, travel, support, and cost required.
